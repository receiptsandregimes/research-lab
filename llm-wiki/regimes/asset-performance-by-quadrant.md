# Asset Performance by Growth × Inflation Quadrant

Companion to [the four-quadrant framework](growth-inflation-quadrants.md).

outcomes (*confidence: medium* — multiple independent sources agree

## Short-answer table

| Quadrant | Favored assets | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Goldilocks** (growth ↑, inflation ↓) | Growth equities (tech, consumer discretionary); investment-grade bonds *(ReSolve: "disinflationary boom" — stocks shine on earnings growth + fatter margins + falling discount rates)* | Long-dated inflation hedges |
| **Reflation** (growth ↑, inflation ↑) | Cyclicals (energy, materials, financials); commodities; emerging-market equities *(ReSolve: EM tend to dominate in inflationary booms — they supply the commodities)*; short-duration bonds (Swoopr) | Long-duration nominal bonds |
| **Stagflation** (growth ↓, inflation ↑) | Commodity-linked assets (energy stocks, materials), inflation-linked bonds, real assets, gold, cash, short duration *(both sides of a 60/40 can lose simultaneously)* | Equities broadly; nominal bonds — the regime that breaks 60/40 |
| **Deflation** (growth ↓, inflation ↓) | Long-duration government bonds (Man Group: "ALWAYS worked in deflationary environments"), quality/defensive equities (utilities, staples, healthcare), cash, gold in severe deflation + financial stress *(ReSolve: gold serves as authorities print to fight deflation)* | Cyclicals; commodities |

Consensus across the three registered sources with verifiable asset
claims (Bridgewater, ReSolve, Swoopr): stocks and nominal bonds both
lose in stagflation — that's the quadrant traditional portfolios are
built to ignore. *(Gavekal's stance can't be verified: our only
registered Gave source makes no asset claims — see Known gaps.)*

## Why each asset behaves this way

Structural mechanics (ReSolve, "Structural Diversification for All
Seasons") — the behavior is embedded in pricing math, not sentiment:

- **Nominal bonds**: price moves opposite rates; rates track inflation
  expectations. Falling inflation → falling rates → rising bond prices.
  Coupons can outweigh price declines in mildly inflationary times.
- **T-bills / cash**: preserves value when inflation is falling;
  T-bills hedge *both* inflation and slowing growth in ReSolve's framing
  ("T-bills hedge against both inflation and slowing growth, which
  validates their relatively large allocation") — hence their combined
  32% of the structurally diversified portfolio. *(Correction, same-day
  QA: the first version credited this to Bridgewater's framing — but the
  All Weather PDF never mentions T-bills; the claim is ReSolve's.)*
- **Equities**: driven above all by *growth* (earnings, book value
  growth), secondarily by inflation expectations (discount rate, input
  costs, margins).
- **Inflation-linked bonds (TIPS)**: designed hedge for rising
  inflation; ~10% of ReSolve's structurally diversified portfolio.
- **Gold, commodities, real estate**: hold value against a weakening
  currency when inflation rises; gold additionally hedges monetary
  devaluation in deflation-fighting episodes.
- **EM equities**: supply-driven boom exposure in inflationary booms.

## Man Group's empirical fine print

Man Group (*The Road Ahead*, May 2026) tested seven regime definitions
against six assets/strategies (equities, bonds, commodities, L/S value,
L/S cross-sectional momentum, trend) with real returns and hit rates.
Key findings that qualify the table above:

- **High and rising inflation: trend-following and commodities have
  "ALWAYS" worked** — "obese pitches." (Note: a *strategy*, not an
  asset.)
- Deflationary environments: **bonds "ALWAYS" worked**; also when
  monetary policy is loosening.
- **Equities "ALMOST ALWAYS" worked** when real growth is booming and
  policy is loosening.
- Bonds were positive (real terms) in only a little more than half of
  equity bear markets — "historically there have been better defensive
  diversifiers than the traditional equity foil."
- **Inflation was the most predictable of the regime frameworks**
  tested — if allowed one economic data series: US headline CPI.
- Equity bull markets are ~80% of history.
- **No magic bullet**: "All regime frameworks... are messy. Things
  kind of work, but not definitively."
- Warning against a myth: **L/S Equity Value does NOT consistently
  benefit from high and rising inflation**, despite doing so in the
  2021–22 episode.

## Known gaps

- All performance claims are directional; no source we've compiled
  reports quadrant-conditional returns with enough precision to
  backtest a tilt. Man Group has the numbers (figures not in the
  article text) but they're proprietary.
- The Gavekal quadrant-specific asset map (in *The General Theory of
  Portfolio Construction*, and in Gave's "Four Quadrants: A Wicksellian
  Analysis") is **not relayed by any registered source** — the
  portfoliooptimizer.io page gives only quadrant definitions and the two
  detector ratios. Primary Gavekal documents not yet registered.
- 2026+ history (AI-productivity boom, post-2022 inflation episode)
  hasn't been quadrant-classified in our sources.

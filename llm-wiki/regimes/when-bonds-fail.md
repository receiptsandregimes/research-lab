# When Bonds Fail, What To Own Instead

Short answer: nominal bonds lose when **inflation is rising** — the
Reflation and Stagflation quadrants. Each bad-bond regime has its own
winners: commodities and cyclicals in reflation; commodities,
inflation-linked bonds, gold, real assets, and cash in stagflation.
Bonds' home turf is the opposite corner: Deflation, where Man Group
finds they "ALWAYS" worked. All claims are historical tendencies, not
guarantees.

## Why bonds lose

Structural mechanics, from ReSolve (via
[asset-performance-by-quadrant](asset-performance-by-quadrant.md)):
bond prices move opposite to interest rates, and rates track inflation
expectations. Rising inflation → rising rates → falling bond prices.
Coupons can offset price declines in *mildly* inflationary times, but
not in a serious inflation regime. Duration amplifies the damage:
long-dated nominal bonds are the most exposed; short-duration bonds
mostly escape (Swoopr lists short-duration bonds as reflation-favored).

## Regime-by-regime: the damage and the alternatives

| Quadrant | Bond damage | What wins instead | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reflation** (growth ↑, inflation ↑) | Long-duration nominal bonds avoid; short-duration okay | Cyclicals (energy, materials, financials), commodities, EM equities (they supply the commodities) | ReSolve, Swoopr |
| **Stagflation** (growth ↓, inflation ↑) | Worst case — nominal bonds lose *and* stocks lose; "the regime that breaks 60/40" | Commodities & commodity-linked equities, inflation-linked bonds (TIPS), real assets, gold, cash, short duration | Bridgewater, ReSolve, Swoopr consensus |
| *(Contrast)* **Deflation** (growth ↓, inflation ↓) | None — bonds' best regime; long government bonds "ALWAYS worked" (Man Group) | Bonds themselves, plus quality/defensive equities, gold in severe deflation | Man Group, ReSolve |

Strategy-level alternatives in high-and-rising inflation (Man Group,
*The Road Ahead*, via
[asset-performance-by-quadrant](asset-performance-by-quadrant.md)):
**trend-following** and commodities "ALWAYS" worked — "obese pitches."
Note trend-following is a strategy, not an asset.

## Bonds are a weaker shield than assumed

Two Man Group findings qualify the reflexive "flee to bonds" instinct:

- Bonds were positive in real terms in only **a little more than half
  of equity bear markets** — "historically there have been better
  defensive diversifiers than the traditional equity foil."
- Myth check: **long/short equity value does NOT consistently benefit
  from high and rising inflation**, despite doing so in 2021–22.

The structural conclusion (ReSolve, echoed by Bridgewater's
surprise-framing): bonds are not a generic safe asset — they are a
*bet on falling inflation*. When inflation is the shock, the hedge has
the shock's own sign.

## Caveats

- Directional tendencies only; no registered source gives
  quadrant-conditional returns precise enough to backtest a tilt (Man
  Group has the numbers but they're proprietary).
- Man Group's meta-verdict on all regime frameworks: "All regime
  frameworks... are messy. Things kind of work, but not definitively."
- Per Swoopr: express this as 5–15 percentage-point tilts with
  multiple indicator confirmations, not wholesale rotations.
- Whether "rising inflation" means raw levels (Gave/ReSolve/Swoopr) or
  surprises vs. priced-in expectations (Bridgewater) is an unresolved
  definitional question that would change any live detector — see
  [the four-quadrant framework](growth-inflation-quadrants.md).

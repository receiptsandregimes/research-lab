# Receipts and Regimes

A public trading-research diary: **Frank** — 30+ years in the markets — and
**Qwen**, his AI research agent.

The premise is simple. We ingest what we read (articles, papers, threads,
transcripts), let it raise questions, and then actually go research those
questions — with price data, primary sources, and honest reasoning — instead
of letting them drift away. Every finding carries a confidence level. Every
confidence level is eventually put on trial by the market.

This repo has two layers:

- **[`llm-wiki/`](llm-wiki/)** — *what we know.* Curated, linked pages that
  compound over time: market regimes, edge, risk, trade post-mortems, and
  the psychology of the game. Start at [`llm-wiki/index.md`](llm-wiki/index.md).
- **[`research/`](research/)** — *how we came to know it.* The working
  diary: open questions, investigations, dead ends, walls we hit, and calls
  we got wrong. When an investigation finishes, its conclusion gets distilled
  up into the wiki; the flailing stays here, on the record.

## House rules

1. **We never delete, we correct.** Wrong calls stay visible. Corrections are
   appended with a note on what we missed. The track record is the point.
2. **Confidence is earned, and it's written down.**
   *Low* — one source or a vibe. *Medium* — several independent sources
   agree, no numbers yet. *High* — primary evidence: data, a test, a
   backable document. Confidence changes are logged, especially the
   embarrassing downgrades.
3. **Everything here is the public record.** Nothing is published without
   Frank's explicit sign-off. Paywalled or licensed material is linked, never
   copied. Personal account details are redacted or shown as ranges.

## The loop

> ingest → questions → research → answers (with confidence) → new questions → repeat

The goal isn't to look smart. It's for Frank to become a better trader, one
documented mistake at a time — and maybe for the receipts to help somebody
else along the way.

*Not financial advice. It's a diary.*

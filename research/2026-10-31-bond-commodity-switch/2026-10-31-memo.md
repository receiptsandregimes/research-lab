# Memo 1 — 2026-10-31: seeding the bond ↔ commodity switch

Origin: conversation. Frank's idea — passively switch, over the long
term, between bonds and commodities using their relative price trend.
Ancestor: Charles Gave's Currency Debasement Ratio (10-year Treasury
total return ÷ gold price, vs its 7-year moving average; >1 →
inflation contained). Frank's variant drops gold and uses AQR's
long-run commodity basket instead. The gold drop is deliberate: gold
is price-fixed at $35/oz before August 1971, so a gold-based ratio is
only testable for ~55 years; the AQR basket runs 1877-02 → 2025-05
(148 years, verified gapless by us on 2026-10-31).

## The hypothesis, split into legs that can fail separately

- **H1 (descriptive):** the bond/basket relative-price trend separates
  the historical record into high- and low-inflation stretches that
  line up with the known inflation history (war spikes, the 1970s,
  the great disinflation, 2021–22). *Falsifier: the ratio's "regime
  calls" disagree with realized inflation episodes more often than
  chance allows.*
- **H2 (timing):** the signal flips near the turns — usable lead/lag,
  not perpetually years late. *Falsifier: signal confirms regimes
  only well after they are visible in raw inflation data, i.e. zero
  timing value over just holding through.*
- **H3 (actionable):** a strategy trading the signal — hold long
  Treasuries when the signal says bonds, the commodity basket when it
  says commodities — beats (a) a static 50/50 blend and (b) naive
  relative momentum ("own last year's winner"), on a risk-adjusted
  basis, **in each era separately**, under stated cost assumptions.
  *Falsifier: loses to either benchmark in either era.*

Splitting matters: H1 can be true (the ratio describes the past) while
H3 is false (you can't harvest it after lag and costs). Only H3 makes
the strategy worth running.

## What prior research already says (web pass, 2026-10-31)

- **No published backtest of this exact rule found.** Gave and
  Portfolio Optimizer present the ratio as a quadrant *indicator*;
  neither publishes strategy results. This is our hypothesis, not
  theirs — we own the burden of proof.
- Closest published precedent located: a university team's macro-regime
  rotation paper (El-Ayari et al., QUANTT, Feb 2026; four regimes from
  growth+inflation z-scores, 1962–2025, walk-forward, beats S&P 500,
  block-bootstrap p=0.013). It tests *indicator-driven* rotation across
  25 ETFs, not a *price-ratio* switch — supports the family of ideas,
  not ours specifically.
- Our own wiki's evidence supports the see-saw direction: AQR —
  basket earns 14.8% high-inflation vs −4.7% low-inflation (premium
  arrives via spot); Man Group — bonds "ALWAYS" worked in deflation,
  commodities "ALWAYS" worked with high and rising inflation.
- Man Group's caveat applies with force here: all regime frameworks
  are "messy"; signals work directionally, not definitively; regimes
  are clearer in hindsight than in real time. That caveat *is* H2.

## Design worries, phrased as constraints on any future test

1. **Apples vs slightly-different-apples.** AQR's index is a futures
   *excess* return series (no cash leg); Shiller's bond return is a
   total return. The strategy must define each leg's full holding
   (e.g., basket + cash earning the short rate vs long Treasuries)
   *before* results exist.
2. **Era split is mandatory.** Pre-1945 the basket is mostly ags and
   meats (6 commodities at the 1877 start; only 7 added before 1945),
   and early prices are high/low averages, not closes. A result that
   only holds in one era must be reported as such.
3. **The parameter is inherited dogma.** Gave's 7-year moving average
   was chosen for gold and for quadrant description, not tuned or
   tested for this use. Any choice of lookback is a config decision
   to make once, before results, and to test sensitivity on.
4. **Gold standard vs fiat.** Pre-1971 the mechanism is different
   (wars debase; the peg holds in peacetime). The ratio may still
   work, but we should not *explain* pre-1971 results with the
   post-1971 monetary-debasement story.
5. **Costs and investability.** The live basket is not investable as
   constructed (AQR acknowledges Erb–Harvey's fringe-commodity
   objection). The backtest is a signal test with cost assumptions,
   not a claim this exact index can be bought.
6. **Whipsaw.** A switch rule trades precisely at trend turns; lag and
   churn are where it dies (this is H2/H3's failure mode).
7. **Sample edges.** AQR ends 2025-05; Shiller runs to 2026-10. Test
   window ends 2025-05. Known file quirks already catalogued in the
   wiki (text dates pre-1900, blank inflation state in last row).

## Test sketch (a sketch, not a spec)

Monthly data 1877-02 → 2025-05. Bond leg: Shiller's synthetic 10-year
Treasury total return (verified). Commodity leg: AQR equal-weight
index return (+ cash convention TBD). Signal: ratio of cumulative
total-return indices vs its moving average, threshold at 1, evaluated
month-end, acted next month (no lookahead). Benchmarks: static 50/50
annual-rebalanced, naive 12-month relative momentum, cash. Report:
full sample + the two eras separately; number of switches; drawdowns
in each era. **No numbers computed in this investigation until this
folder's protocol is locked.**
